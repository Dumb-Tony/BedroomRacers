# 00 — Vision

## High concept

Bedroom Racers is a colourful arcade racing game in which players control toy
vehicles across oversized environments inspired by childhood play spaces.

The flagship environment is a bedroom floor covered by a nostalgic town-map rug.
Roads, buildings, intersections, bridges and parking areas printed on the rug form
the basis of the race tracks. Everyday bedroom objects become enormous obstacles,
landmarks, shortcuts and hazards.

## Player fantasy

The player should feel like a child bringing a toy-car world to life:

- Racing toy cars across a bedroom floor
- Inventing routes through household objects
- Discovering hidden shortcuts
- Performing exaggerated jumps and drifts
- Unlocking a collection of distinctive toy vehicles
- Turning ordinary childhood spaces into epic racing environments

The scale relationship is the whole point. Every frame should remind you that these
cars are three inches long and the pencil in front of them is a fallen tree.

## Design pillars

Use these to settle arguments. If a feature doesn't serve a pillar, it's probably
scope creep.

### 1. Childhood imagination
Every environment is a familiar childhood space transformed into a large-scale
adventure. Recognisability comes first — a player should identify the space in
under a second, then be delighted by what's been done to it.

### 2. Accessible arcade racing
Controls are immediately understandable. Depth comes from drifting, shortcuts,
vehicle choice and track knowledge — not from control complexity. An eight-year-old
should be able to finish a race on their first try.

### 3. Interactive toy worlds
Objects affect gameplay. Blocks fall, pencils roll, books form ramps, toy trains
cross the track. A world that only looks like a bedroom is a failure; it has to
behave like one being played in.

### 4. Replayable tracks
Tracks offer alternate routes, collectibles, secrets, time-trial goals and changing
hazards. The second lap should teach you something the first didn't.

### 5. Expandable structure
New environments, vehicles, race modes and track pieces can be added without
rebuilding core systems. This is an engineering pillar with design consequences:
content is data, not code.

## Audience

**Primary:** Players aged 8+ who enjoy accessible arcade racing.

**Secondary:** Adults nostalgic for 1990s bedroom toys — town-map play rugs,
die-cast cars, plastic racing tracks, toy boxes, classic arcade racers.

The secondary audience is a genuine target, not an afterthought. The art direction
and audio should land emotionally for someone who owned one of these rugs, while
never requiring that memory to enjoy the game.

**Rating:** Family-friendly. No realistic violence, no gambling mechanics, no
aggressive monetisation.

## Tone

Warm, playful, slightly wistful. Collisions are funny, not punishing. The framing
is that the toys come alive when nobody's watching — there's a gentle secrecy to it.

Avoid: snark, edginess, ironic detachment, anything that reads as a toy commercial.

## Unique selling points

- A nostalgic toy-car fantasy executed with real craft
- Tracks built from familiar childhood environments
- A town-map rug as the flagship racing world
- Interactive household objects
- Accessible browser-based gameplay with no install
- A modular structure supporting future themed environments

## What this game is not

- Not a simulation. Physics serve feel, not realism.
- Not an open world. Tracks are authored, bounded and readable.
- Not a live-service game. No energy systems, no daily login economy.
- Not a licensed toy game. Inspiration is generic and legally clean.
