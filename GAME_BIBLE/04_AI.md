# 04 — AI

## Approach

**Waypoint following on authored racing lines.** No pathfinding, no navmesh, no
steering-behaviour stack. This is a deliberate scope decision — AI navigation is a
listed project risk, and waypoint AI on a bounded track is a solved problem.

AI drivers use the **same vehicle physics as the player**. They do not cheat with
different acceleration curves or ignore surfaces. Difficulty comes from decision
quality and input precision, not from separate rules.

This matters: if an AI car can do something the player's car cannot, players notice,
and the racing stops feeling fair.

## Racing lines

Each track defines one or more racing lines as ordered waypoint lists.

```js
{
  id: "main",
  waypoints: [
    { x: 420, y: 180, targetSpeed: 1.0, width: 46 },
    { x: 610, y: 210, targetSpeed: 0.7, width: 38 },
    // ...
  ]
}
```

- `targetSpeed` — fraction of the vehicle's max speed. Authored per corner.
- `width` — lateral tolerance. Wide sections let AI spread out and race each other;
  narrow sections force single file.

Alternate lines (shortcuts, high-risk routes) are separate lists with entry and exit
waypoints that rejoin the main line. See `05_Tracks.md`.

## Driver loop

Each frame, an AI driver:

1. Finds its target waypoint (current + lookahead scaled by speed).
2. Steers toward it, with lateral offset for personality and traffic avoidance.
3. Brakes if approaching a waypoint whose `targetSpeed` is below current speed.
4. Drifts if the required turn rate exceeds what grip allows.
5. Decides on boost (see below).
6. Checks for obstacles ahead; nudges the lateral offset to avoid.
7. Detects being stuck; triggers recovery.

### Step 6 was specified for six phases before it existed

It went unbuilt and unnoticed because every bedroom track keeps its props at the
edges, where a driver on the line never meets one. Putting buckets in the road on
Bucket Brigade found it in a single race: the field drove into them lap after lap.

| | before | after | baseline (rug) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bucket Brigade | 124.5s, 27 impacts, 2029 stuck ticks | 91.6s, 8.7, 206 | 92.5s, 6, 105 |
| The Big Dig | 92.2s, 14.3, 601 | 80.4s, 5.3, 185 | — |

`avoidProps()` shifts the desired lateral offset until it misses everything ahead,
picking whichever side needs less movement so a driver already going one way
commits rather than dithering across the middle, and clamping to the road so it
never dodges into a wall. Props being jumped — `v.z > p.clearAt` — are ignored,
which is what lets a driver launched off a ramp sail over a bucket instead of
swerving round one it was about to clear.

**The Big Dig also exposed a bug in `sandReading`.** The scan asked for a full sand
cell either side regardless of the road; on a 230-wide trench with unjumpable
walls that had the driver aiming 70 units off the line with 115 to play with, and
grinding along the wall for a 63-second lap. `SAND_CLEARANCE` now keeps the scan
off the kerb, and a road too narrow for a full-cell scan returns no opinion at all
rather than a confident wrong one. Wide tracks had hidden it completely.

## Personalities

Personalities are **parameter sets**, not separate code paths. Each is a modifier
bundle over the base driver.

| Personality | Behaviour | Key parameters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Speedster** | Very fast on straights, frequently crashes | High `targetSpeedMul`, late braking, low `cornerCaution` |
| **The Technician** | Clean lines, efficient drifts | High `lineAccuracy`, optimal drift timing, high `boostEfficiency` |
| **The Bully** | Heavy vehicle, pushes others aside | High `aggression`, seeks contact, ignores lateral avoidance |
| **The Explorer** | Frequently takes alternate routes | High `shortcutBias`, accepts higher risk |
| **The Rookie** | Slow and forgiving, for beginner events | Low `targetSpeedMul`, early braking, rare boost use |

```js
const PERSONALITY = {
  targetSpeedMul:  1.0,   // scales authored corner speeds
  lineAccuracy:    0.8,   // 0..1, how tightly the line is held
  cornerCaution:   1.0,   // braking distance multiplier
  aggression:      0.3,   // willingness to make contact
  shortcutBias:    0.3,   // probability of taking an alternate route
  boostEfficiency: 0.7,   // how well boost is timed
  mistakeChance:   0.05,  // per-corner chance of a deliberate error
};
```

**Deliberate mistakes are a feature.** An AI that never errs is unbeatable-feeling
even when slow. `mistakeChance` produces overshoots and missed apexes that let
players pass and feel good about it.

### Reading the surface — `sandReading`

A ninth parameter, added with Sandbox Speedway. It is the only trait that reads
the **world** rather than the authored line: on sand, the driver scans across the
road and steers toward ground that has already been packed down (`SandGrid.js`).
Inert everywhere else, because the grid is inactive on non-sandy tracks.

| Personality | `sandReading` | reading it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technician | 0.95 | reading the surface *is* the personality |
| Bully | 0.55 | will happily take the line you just made |
| Speedster | 0.45 | too busy going fast to look down |
| Rookie | 0.15 | hasn't noticed the ground is different |

**This does not break the no-cheating rule.** Compaction is drawn on screen, so
the AI is looking at the ground exactly as a player does. It gets no lap times,
no opponent state, and no lookahead the player lacks.

Measured on Dune Dash, comparing laps 2-3 against the same race with the trait
zeroed: Technician −1.0s, Bully −0.5s, Speedster −0.6s, Rookie −0.2s. The gain
tracks the parameter, which is what tells you the trait is doing the work and
not something else.

A side effect worth keeping: it **breaks up single file**. The Technician's
lateral spread went from 1.3 to 23.1 units, because it now picks a line instead
of tracing one. The wander in step 2 of the driver loop exists to stop the field
running nose-to-tail; on sand this does the same job for a reason.

**Sizing the scan is not free.** The first implementation sampled a fraction of
the racing line's usable width — 134 units on Dune Dash — which put the entire
five-point scan inside a single 70-unit sand cell. Every sample returned the same
number, half the reads came back "no opinion", and the AI drove straight past
packed sand it was alongside. **A scan narrower than one grid cell cannot resolve
anything by construction.** The radius is now taken from `SandGrid.CELL`, not
from the road.

## Difficulty

Difficulty scales the personality bundle, it does not replace it. A Speedster on
Easy is still recognisably a Speedster.

| | Easy | Normal | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opponent speed | Reduced | Balanced | Faster lines |
| Shortcut use | Rare | Moderate | Aggressive |
| Boost timing | Poor | Decent | Effective |
| Player recovery | Stronger | Standard | Smaller advantage |
| Collision handling | More forgiving | Standard | Standard |
| `mistakeChance` | Elevated | Baseline | Low |

**Player-selectable from Phase 5**, and the player's choice overrides whatever an
event was authored with. Pacing across the roster is carried by lap count and
grid size, which a difficulty setting does not touch, so nothing is lost by
letting the setting win.

"More forgiving collision handling" and "stronger player recovery" are one
mechanism: a per-vehicle `forgiveness` multiplier, applied to the **player only**.
Opponents keep normal physics on every setting, so Easy reads as the player being
tougher rather than as rivals being limp.

**Platinum is not awarded on Easy.** Gold and below are unaffected — the point of
an easier setting is to be able to finish and progress — but the mastery tier has
to mean the same thing for everyone.

## Dynamic assistance

> **Built in Phase 5.** Trailing AI get up to +6% target speed, scaled by how far
> back they are. A leading car is **never** slowed — the multiplier is clamped at
> or above 1.0, verified. A trailing player earns boost slightly faster. The
> whole system is disabled in Time Trial.

Subtle rubber-banding for players who fall far behind. Must never be detectable.

Rules:

- **Only applies when the player is behind**, never to hold a leading player back.
  Slowing a winning player is the single most resented mechanic in arcade racing.
- Trailing AI receive a small speed bonus so the pack does not string out
  indefinitely; leading AI receive **no** penalty.
- The player's own boost regeneration may increase slightly when far behind.
- Maximum effect should be small enough that a skilled player still wins comfortably
  and a struggling player still loses — it compresses the field, it does not decide
  the race.
- **Disabled entirely in Time Trial** and any mode with leaderboards.

Suggested cap: ±6% effective speed. Tune with real players; err low.

## Stuck detection and recovery

An AI is stuck if it has not advanced a waypoint for ~2 seconds while grounded.

Recovery: reverse briefly, re-aim at the current waypoint, resume. If still stuck
after a second attempt, respawn at the last checkpoint exactly as the player would.
Never let an AI grind against a wall for a whole lap — it looks broken and it is.

## Overtaking

Lightweight. When an AI is within about two car lengths of a slower vehicle ahead:

1. Pick a side with more track width available.
2. Apply a lateral offset to the racing line.
3. Hold the offset until clear, then rejoin.
4. If blocked and `aggression` is high, make contact instead.

No cooperative traffic model, no lane reservation. Overtaking that occasionally
fails is more entertaining than overtaking that always works.

## Open questions

1. **How many AI opponents?** GDD says 3 for the prototype, 5 for the sample event.
   Performance and readability both cap this; find the number where the pack still
   reads clearly on a mobile screen.
2. **Should AI use items** once items exist (`10_Items.md`)? Item AI is a separate
   design problem and may need its own decision layer.
3. **Ghost recording format** — Time Trial ghosts need deterministic playback. This
   constrains the timestep decision in `03_Driving_Physics.md`.
