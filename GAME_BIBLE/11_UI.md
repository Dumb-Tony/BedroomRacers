# 11 — User Interface

## Principles

- **Colourful, legible, unobtrusive.** In that order of priority when they conflict.
- **Readable at mobile size first.** If it works on a phone it works everywhere.
- **Diegetic where possible.** Stickers, notebook labels, cardboard signs — not
  glass-and-neon sci-fi panels.
- **No text where an icon will do**, especially for the 8-year-old audience.
- **Nothing obscures the racing line.** HUD lives in corners.

## Visual style

UI elements may resemble stickers, toy packaging, notebook labels, crayon drawings,
plastic buttons, cardboard signs.

The unifying idea: the interface looks like it was made by the same child who owns
the toys — hand-cut, hand-lettered, slightly wonky. Nothing perfectly aligned.

## HUD

During a race:

| Element | Position | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Position** | Top left | Large. `3rd` / `1st`. The most-read element |
| **Lap count** | Top left, under position | `LAP 2/3` |
| **Timer** | Top centre | Current time; lap split flashes on lap completion |
| **Boost meter** | Bottom centre or near vehicle | Must be readable without looking away from the car |
| **Item slot** | Top right | Empty when items are disabled |
| **Mini-map** | Bottom right | Track outline, player dot, opponent dots |
| **Objective** | Contextual | Only when relevant to the mode |

### Boost meter placement

The hardest HUD decision. A corner-mounted meter forces the eye off the car at
exactly the moment precision matters.

Options: attached to the vehicle (a ring or trailing bar), bottom-centre near the
natural eye position, or both — a subtle vehicle-attached cue plus a precise corner
readout.

**Recommend a vehicle-attached primary cue.** Resolve during Phase 1, since drift
charge feedback is part of whether drifting feels good.

### Next-corner indicator

Bottom-centre, just under the car — corners are read while looking at the road,
not at the corner of the screen. A curved arrow shows which way the road bends,
how sharply, and a bar that fills as it arrives.

**Derived from the racing line the AI already uses.** Every waypoint carries a
`targetSpeed` computed from curvature (`05_Tracks.md`), so severity comes free
and cannot disagree with the track — there is no second copy of the corner data
to fall out of sync.

- Colour and shape by severity: green, amber, red. Only the worst tier gets a
  word; labelling every bend is noise.
- It fades in as the corner comes into range, so a distant one does not shout.
- Nothing is drawn on a straight.
- Distance is a **filling bar, not a number** — world units mean nothing to a
  player.

It earns its place because of the camera: at 17.5° you can see a long way ahead
but the ground is compressed to 30%, so a corner reads late. This buys that time
back.

Verified against both tracks: **41 corners, 100% direction agreement** with the
geometry measured independently, severity tracking the real bend angle (a −82°
turn reads as a hairpin, −24° as easy), nothing shown on the longest straight,
and each viewport in a split tracking its own driver.

### Callouts

Large, brief, animated text over the action. Toy-sticker styling.

`Great Drift!` · `Shortcut!` · `Perfect Landing!` · `Final Lap!` · `New Best Time!`

Callouts are the cheap version of an announcer (see `13_Audio.md`) and should work
with sound off.

## Screens

### Main menu
Play · Garage · Track Select · Collection · Settings · Credits

Presented as objects in the room rather than a list where practical.

### Garage
See `09_Vehicles.md`. A toy shelf or carrying case.

### Track select
The bedroom seen from above. Physical areas act as regions: town rug, under the bed,
toy box, desk, window area, closet, and future environments.

Event nodes sit on the map. Locked content is visible but greyed — players should see
what's coming.

### Results
Finishing position · race time · best lap · medal earned · stars completed ·
collectibles found · rewards unlocked · **Retry** · **Next event**

Retry must be the most prominent button and must be reachable in one press. Per
`01_Game_Loop.md`, retry-to-countdown is targeted under 2 seconds.

### Pause
Resume · Restart · Settings · Quit to menu. Available at any time, including mid-air
and during the countdown.

## Accessibility

Not optional, and not deferred. Several of these are load-bearing for the 8+ audience.

- Adjustable sound levels (music and SFX separately)
- Reduced screen shake
- **Reduced camera rotation** — see below
- Reduced flashing effects
- Colourblind-friendly indicators
- High-contrast UI mode
- Remappable controls
- Auto-accelerate (default on)
- Steering assistance
- Difficulty selection
- Subtitle support
- Pause at any time

### Notes

**Colourblind indicators** matter more than usual here — vehicles are identified by
colour name (Red Racer, Blue Buggy). Never rely on colour alone for position
indicators or opponent identification; pair with shape or number.

**Steering assistance** should be a gentle nudge away from walls, not auto-steering.
Aim it at the youngest players without making the game play itself.

**Reduced camera rotation** is new, and matters more than it sounds. The chase
camera rotates the whole world around the car (`03_Driving_Physics.md`). A
rotating view is harder to parse for younger players and is a known motion-sickness
trigger. The option should clamp or disable yaw rotation, falling back toward a
fixed-orientation view. Cheap to build — the projection already takes yaw as a
parameter, so the setting is a clamp on one number.

**Reduced flashing** interacts with boost effects, callouts and the holiday
string-lights lighting state.

## Onboarding

Track 1 is the tutorial (`06_World_Town_Rug.md`). Teaching is diegetic — printed
signs and rug arrows, not modal popups.

Rule: **a player who ignores every prompt must still be able to finish the race.**
Instruction is an aid, never a gate.

## Open questions

1. Boost meter placement (above). Phase 1.
2. **Partly resolved.** ~~Does the mini-map rotate with the camera or stay
   north-up?~~ **It stays north-up.** The world already rotates around the car,
   so a rotating map would show the same orientation as the screen and tell the
   player nothing new — while leaving no fixed reference anywhere in the
   interface. Held fixed it does the one job the rotating view cannot: shows
   where you are in the circuit as a whole.

   The player is an **arrowhead, not a dot**, so facing is readable. Opponents
   are dots in their own body colours, matching how they are identified on
   track. The toy train is shown deliberately — it is meant to be timed, not
   stumbled into.

   **Both now exist.** The next-corner indicator is built too, and both have a
   slider reaching zero, so which earns its screen space is answerable by
   driving rather than arguing.

   They turned out **not to be alternatives**. The map answers *where am I in
   the lap*; the corner hint answers *what happens in the next second*. Nothing
   yet says a track needs both, but they are not competing for the same job.
3. Menu navigation model — mouse/touch only, or full keyboard and gamepad support
   from the start? Gamepad menu support is easy early and painful to retrofit.
